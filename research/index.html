Research

Singularity formation and long-time dynamics in fluid and dispersive equations.

My research concerns singularity formation and long-time dynamics in nonlinear partial differential equations, with contributions to fluid mechanics and nonlinear wave equations. A common theme is dynamical selection: which singular or coherent regimes emerge under nonlinear evolution, and why do they persist under perturbations? I develop energy, spectral, semigroup, and modulation methods to describe the rates, profiles, and stability of these regimes.

Structure and stability in fluid equations

My work on the De Gregorio modification of the Constantin–Lax–Majda model studies the interplay between convection and vortex stretching. With Zhen Lei and Xiao Ren, I proved global well-posedness for sign-definite vorticity without a smallness assumption, using a new conservation law. We also established exponential stability near the ground-state family and removed a mean-zero restriction through an adapted energy space. This work illustrates how the choice of function space and energy structure can reveal stability hidden in the original equation.

With Ke Chen, I constructed finite-energy weak solutions of three-dimensional hypoviscous incompressible elastodynamics using convex integration. Together, these results address both nonlinear stability and the flexibility of weak solutions in fluid-related systems.

Related papers: vorticity dynamics and stability and weak solutions of hypoviscous elastodynamics.

Singularity formation and asymptotic stability

I study which blow-up profiles and rates are selected by nonlinear evolution and whether they remain stable under perturbations. In joint work with Tej-eddine Ghoul and Nader Masmoudi on a wave equation with quadratic spatial-derivative nonlinearity, we ruled out smooth exact self-similar blow-up profiles, constructed generalized self-similar solutions with logarithmic growth, and proved their asymptotic stability. The analysis combines spectral and semigroup estimates for non-self-adjoint operators with modulation and nonlinear energy bounds.

In my solo work on superconformal semilinear wave equations, I used Lorentz transformations in similarity variables to establish spectral equivalence across a family of blow-up profiles and prove nonlinear asymptotic stability. Joint work with Faiq Raees extends the generalized self-similar analysis to models with quadratic time-derivative nonlinearities.

Related papers: generalized self-similar blow-up, Lorentz symmetry and stability, and time-derivative models.

Energy transfer, radiation, and scattering

With Zhen Lei and Zhaojie Yang, I studied how nonlinear resonance transfers energy from localized internal modes to radiation in Hamiltonian nonlinear Klein–Gordon equations with a potential. Our work derives sharp damping laws in the weak-resonance regime and treats interactions among multiple, possibly degenerate internal modes. The analysis brings together generalized Fermi golden rules, normal-form transformations, cancellation structures, and dispersive estimates. These results connect metastability and radiation damping with the broader question of dynamical selection.

Earlier joint work with Daoyin He and Keyan Wang established a precise scattering profile for two-dimensional quasilinear wave equations satisfying null conditions.

Related papers: weak resonance and damping, multiple internal modes, and two-dimensional scattering.
