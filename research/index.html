Research

Singularity formation and long-time dynamics in fluid and dispersive equations.

My research concerns singularity formation and long-time dynamics in fluid and dispersive equations. I study how transport, nonlinear focusing, resonance, and radiation shape the evolution of solutions, and how these mechanisms select singular or coherent structures that persist under perturbations.

Boundary layer separation and turbulence

Boundary-layer separation and the generation of fine scales are important mechanisms in fluid motion. My interests include the dynamics of unsteady separation, the amplification and transport of vorticity, and their connections with turbulent motion and the interaction between viscous and inviscid flow.

Vorticity dynamics and fluid stability

The competition between transport, stretching, and dissipation governs the evolution of vorticity and the stability of fluid structures. My work on vorticity models examines how nonlinear structure and energy balance control these dynamics. Related work on hypoviscous elastodynamics explores the behavior and flexibility of finite-energy weak solutions in coupled fluid systems.

Compressible focusing and collapse

Compression and attractive forces can concentrate mass and produce large velocity gradients in compressible flow. I am interested in the mechanisms that select collapse profiles and rates, the competition between pressure and focusing, and the stability of these regimes under perturbations.

Self-similar blow-up in nonlinear waves

Nonlinear focusing can lead to finite-time blow-up, with self-similar or generalized self-similar profiles describing the emerging scales. My work on nonlinear wave equations studies these blow-up regimes and their asymptotic stability, including the influence of symmetry and derivative nonlinearities on the singular dynamics.

Resonance, radiation, and energy transfer

Nonlinear resonance allows localized coherent modes to exchange energy with radiation, producing damping and metastable behavior. My work on Hamiltonian Klein–Gordon equations examines these interactions and the resulting long-time dynamics. Related work on wave scattering studies how nonlinear solutions approach freely propagating waves.
