Research

Singularity formation and long-time dynamics in fluid and dispersive equations.

My research lies in the analysis of nonlinear partial differential equations, especially fluid equations and nonlinear wave equations. I study how solutions form singularities, how singular behavior persists under perturbations, and how solutions evolve over long times.

Singularity formation and stability

I investigate self-similar blow-up and its stability for nonlinear wave equations, including equations with derivative nonlinearities. My work includes stability of self-similar solutions for superconformal semilinear wave equations and blow-up for wave equations with quadratic spatial or time-derivative nonlinearities.

Related papers: superconformal semilinear waves, quadratic spatial derivatives, and quadratic time derivatives.

Long-time dynamics and energy transfer

I study resonances, energy transfer, and radiation damping in Hamiltonian nonlinear Klein–Gordon equations, as well as scattering for quasilinear wave equations satisfying null conditions. An important theme is the interaction between discrete and continuous spectral modes and the role of Fermi’s golden rule.

Related papers: weak resonance, multiple internal modes, and two-dimensional scattering.

Fluid models and weak solutions

My work on fluid-related equations includes the Constantin–Lax–Majda model with convection and weak solutions of three-dimensional hypoviscous elastodynamics. These problems concern regularity, stability, and nonuniqueness in nonlinear evolution equations.

Related papers: the Constantin–Lax–Majda model and hypoviscous elastodynamics.
